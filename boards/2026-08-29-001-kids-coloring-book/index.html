Consensus

  • The blank interview is the finding. Every seat notes all five diligence questions came back unanswered. All research to date is supply-side (royalty cliff, page specs, line quality); no niche is named, no demand data exists. Codex's phrase — "a production specification pretending to be a business" — was echoed independently by claude, groq, and nvidia.
  • Discoverability, not production, is the binding constraint. A zero-review listing is invisible organically, and at ~$4.50/sale the break-even CPC is roughly $0.44 at 10% conversion (codex and claude computed this independently) — ads are structurally break-even to negative.
  • Defer the character universe. Six seats call it a second unvalidated business (media production) stacked on the first, with no audience or demonstrated video capability. Near-unanimous: niche-book validation first, if anything.
  • Validate before producing. Multiple seats converge on the same free test: pull top-10 comps' BSRs and review counts for one named niche in an afternoon, before generating a single page.
  • Cap the experiment. Claude, codex, and groq independently propose similar bounds: one title, ~20–30 hours, $100–500, with a predefined signal (~10 non-friend sales in 30 days).

Live disagreements

  • QC cost — the model-breaking dispute. Groq (first-principles) estimates ~75 labor hours (~$1,500) per book; mistral agrees labor makes per-unit economics "impossible." Claude counters that connected-line detection is an automatable flood-fill script, putting a book at 8–15 hours falling to ~4; codex lands at 5–8 hours. A timed run settles this.
  • The $4.50 number itself. Claude computes $3.69 net at $9.99 (after $2.30 print cost); codex gets ~$4.39 (assuming ~$1.60 printing); mistral says just run the KDP calculator. The brief's headline number is contested.
  • Short-form video. Mistral (distribution) says the idea only works if you commit to TikTok/Reels; groq (distribution) goes further — run a 60-day TikTok test before publishing. Claude, codex, and gemini call video a motivation bomb to reject until a plain book sells.
  • First experiment. Claude wants a Pinterest free-printables test; codex wants thumbnail-only Amazon ads against mock listings; mistral wants a $20 live-listing ad test; groq (red-team) says spend $0 until a CAC spreadsheet exists.
  • Price. Mistral (capital-allocator) would test $7.99 below the cliff; mistral (distribution) claims specific niches (e.g., neurodivergent-kids) support $9.99+; red-team seats call $9.99 a conversion penalty against $4.99–7.99 norms.

Kill criteria

Drop this idea if any of the following is observed:

  1. The named niche's top-10 comps show BSRs above ~80–100k or review velocity implying incumbents sell under ~300/month.
  2. Verified KDP margin at $9.99 comes in under ~$4.00/sale and comps cluster below $8.
  3. A timed 10-page generate+QC run exceeds ~8 minutes per accepted page even with automated line-checking.
  4. A capped ad probe ($50–100) shows ACOS persistently above ~80% or break-even CPC unattainable.
  5. Fewer than 10 non-friend sales in 30 days post-launch under the capped budget.

Next actions

  1. Name one intersection niche and pull comps — top-10 BSRs, prices, review counts into a sheet (one afternoon, $0). This answers Q1 and kill criterion 1.
  2. Run the exact 50pp single-sided spec through the KDP royalty calculator to settle $4.50 vs. $3.69 (five minutes).
  3. Do a timed 10-page generate+QC batch, logging minutes per accepted page and testing an automated connected-line check — this resolves the 75-hour-vs-8-hour dispute that decides the whole cost model.

Dissent

By: codex

  • The “consensus” is overstated. Cerebras produced nothing usable, Nvidia only partial takes, and Gemini was repeatedly truncated. Correlated agreement among the remaining models—and possibly multiple personas from one model—is not independent confirmation.
  • “The blank interview is the finding” romanticizes missing work. Unanswered diligence questions establish ignorance, not evidence against or for the business.
  • Discoverability is declared the binding constraint without keyword, traffic, conversion, or niche data. “Zero-review listings are invisible” is an unsupported absolute. The $0.44 CPC conclusion rests on an assumed 10% conversion rate and an unsettled royalty figure, so its apparent precision is bogus.
  • The proposed comp test is weak. BSR is volatile, category-dependent, and only indirectly convertible to sales. Review velocity is not sales velocity. Looking only at today’s top ten creates survivor bias and misses keyword volume, seasonality, listing age, ad density, and publisher portfolios.
  • Kill criterion 1 may reject opportunity. Weak incumbent sales can mean poor demand—or underserved demand. The test cannot distinguish them.
  • The experiment lacks statistical power. Ten sales in 30 days says little about repeatability; a $50–100 ad probe may produce too few clicks or purchases to estimate conversion or CAC. “Persistently above 80% ACOS” is especially incoherent: with roughly $4 contribution on $9.99 revenue, break-even ACOS is about 40%, not 80%.
  • QC timing does not “decide the whole cost model.” It excludes niche research, prompt iteration, rejected generations, layout, cover design, metadata, revisions, upload, proof copies, and customer issues.
  • “Name one niche” invites cherry-picking. Score several niches using predefined criteria before selecting one.
  • Major omissions: current KDP AI-content rules, trademark/IP screening, accessibility and sensitivity risks around niches such as neurodivergent children, proof-copy quality, returns, account-policy risk, and opportunity cost.
  • Survives: defer the character universe, verify the royalty, and run a small timed production test before committing.
